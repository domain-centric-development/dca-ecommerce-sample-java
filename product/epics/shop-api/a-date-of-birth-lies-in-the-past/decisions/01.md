# How does the rule's new wording reach the spec when the story no longer carries the two scenarios under it?

## Question

The story renames the rule "The date of birth must be a real date, and not in the future" to "The date of birth must
be a real date in the past" (`## Changed expectations`, line 2). The answered assumption says it no longer carries
`unreadable-date-is-refused` and `future-date-is-refused`: they hold as the spec has them, and the spec moves both
under the renamed rule. The test gate's `spec-delta` check refuses that plan: a renamed rule leaves both criteria
under the old wording unless the story names each as a criterion under the new rule, or the plan `removes` it.
The plan cannot do either: the story file is the person's, and removing the two criteria contradicts the answer.

## Options
- a: The story carries both scenarios again, word for word, under the new rule, mapped to their existing tests.
  This is the gate's own suggestion. It may then meet the test gate's red run with two tests that are green
  today; whether `holds` exempts them is the pipeline's to say.
- b: This story leaves the rule's words alone, and its two new scenarios stand under a rule heading of their own.
  The spec briefly shows two date rules. A later story or task rewords the old one and drops the duplicate.
- c: The pipeline learns to carry held criteria along with a renamed rule (a `spec-delta` change in
  dca-factory). The story waits until then and is planned again as it stands.

## Recommendation
a — it keeps the person's intent, since the rule is renamed and the scenarios are unchanged, and it uses what the gate
already offers. If the red run refuses the two green tests, c is the fix at the source.

## Evidence
- `product/spec/account/registration/scenarios.md:87` — the rule and its two criteria (lines 89, 95)
- story `## Assumptions`, third line — the answer that drops both scenarios from the story
- `.dca-factory/runs/a-date-of-birth-lies-in-the-past/plan.md` `## Spec delta` — the refused lines

## Answer
answer: b — the rule "The date of birth must be a real date, and not in the future" keeps its words; the two new scenarios stand under a rule of their own, "The date of birth must be a real date in the past", as the .NET shop delivered it. A later task rewords the old rule and joins the two in both shops.
