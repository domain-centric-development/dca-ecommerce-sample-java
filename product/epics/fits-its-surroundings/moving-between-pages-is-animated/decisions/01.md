# Reduced motion still moves the product image — adopt the scenario as it is, take it out, or build it?

## Question

The story is adopted: it describes behaviour the shop has. `reduced-motion-means-no-animation` says the product page
"appears without any animation" for a visitor who prefers reduced motion. Observed in Chromium (Playwright, reduced
motion emulated), following "View Details" to "Domain-Driven Design": the page content's fade and slide are gone, but
every transition group still runs the browser's 250 ms morph — `::view-transition-group(root|breadcrumb|site-header|
site-logo|site-footer|product-img-<id>)`. Most of them do not move; the product image does: it glides from the card to
its place on the product page. The scenario's `Then` does not hold today, and an adoption cannot build it.

Cause: `src/main/resources/static/css/main.css:3856` stops `::view-transition-old(*)` and `::view-transition-new(*)`
only; `*` matches no view-transition pseudo-element, so `::view-transition-group(*)` keeps its animation.

## Options

1. **Take the scenario out of this story** — adopt the other three (each has a green characterization test and its
   break) and write a new story that stills the groups under reduced motion as well
   (`::view-transition-group(*) { animation: none !important; }` in the reduced-motion block). The .NET sample shares
   `main.css` and would take it over.
2. **Reword the scenario to today's shop** — "Then the page content appears without a fade; the product image still
   moves to its place" — and adopt it with that.
3. **Make this story a build story** (`status` no longer `adopted`): the test stays red and the build stage adds the
   rule above.

## Recommendation

Option 1. The three scenarios that hold are adopted as they are; the gap is an accessibility defect, and option 2 would
write it into the spec as agreed behaviour. A story of its own keeps the replay honest and the fix visible.

## Evidence

- `src/test-e2e/java/dev/domaincentric/sample/ecommerce/e2e/PageTransitionE2ETest.java` —
  `theProductPageAppearsWithoutAnimationWhenTheVisitorPrefersReducedMotion`, red on today's code with the six group
  animations listed above.


## Answer
answer: A story of its own — the scenario holds as written. Both shops get the change story reduced-motion-stills-the-transitions (no view-transition group animates under prefers-reduced-motion), which this story depends on. This story stays adopted as written, all four scenarios.
