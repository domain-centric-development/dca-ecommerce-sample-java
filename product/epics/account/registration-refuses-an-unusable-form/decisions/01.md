# Four adopted scenarios name sentences the register page does not show — how should the story change?

## Question

The story is `status: adopted`, so every scenario must describe behaviour the shop already has. Three hold:
`differing-passwords-are-refused`, `short-password-is-refused`, `unreadable-date-is-refused`. Four do not. For them
the page answers with the one sentence for an argument exception, "Please check the email address, your name and
your date of birth.", and not with the sentence the scenario names:

| Scenario | Story expects | Page shows today |
|---|---|---|
| future-date-is-refused | Date of birth cannot be in the future | Please check the email address, your name and your date of birth. |
| malformed-email-is-refused | Invalid email format: anna.schmidt@example | the same |
| blank-first-name-is-refused | First name is required | the same |
| overlong-last-name-is-refused | Last name is too long | the same |

The sentences the story expects are the exception messages of `Owner`, `Email` and `UsableDateOfBirth`. The
project's wording rule says such a message is for the log and never reaches a page.

## Options

1. **Reword the four scenarios to what the page shows.** Each expects "Please check the email address, your name
   and your date of birth.", and the story stays adopted. The rule headings stay; the sentences are the same for
   all four.
2. **Take the four scenarios out of this story into a new story that is built, not adopted.** That story makes the
   register page name the field it refuses. It needs typed failures (or a field-level outcome), new rows in
   `docs/architecture/refusal-wording.md`, and the same change in the .NET twin, which carries that file
   unchanged. Its sentences are worded by the adapter, so "Invalid email format: {x}" would be rewritten as a
   page sentence.
3. **Make this whole story a build story (not adopted).** This is the same work as option 2, but the three
   scenarios that already hold could get no red test, so the test gate would refuse them.

## Recommendation

Option 2. The three scenarios that hold are adopted now. The visitor's wish to be told "exactly which entry"
becomes a story of its own. That story carries the wording decision in `refusal-wording.md` that both shops share.
Option 1 is the smaller change, but it contradicts the story's own "so that".

## Evidence

- `src/main/java/dev/domaincentric/sample/ecommerce/account/adapter/incoming/web/RegisterPageController.java:133`: `IllegalArgumentException` is caught.
- `src/main/java/dev/domaincentric/sample/ecommerce/account/adapter/incoming/web/RegisterPageController.java:175`: the one generic sentence.
- `docs/architecture/refusal-wording.md:74`: register, argument exception → the generic sentence.
- `docs/architecture/refusal-wording.md:4`: the exception's message never reaches a page.
- `src/main/java/dev/domaincentric/sample/ecommerce/account/domain/model/Owner.java:58`, `Email.java:33`, `UsableDateOfBirth.java:42`: the messages the story quotes.

## Answer
answer: 1 — the four scenarios expect the page's sentence for an argument exception, "Please check the email address, your name and your date of birth.", and the story's sentence says the page names what to check, not which entry. It stays adopted; the story text is already changed, in both shops alike. Naming the field is not part of this story.
